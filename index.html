How the prototype maps to the model

Three layers

LayerWhat it isWhere you see it in this prototypeProposed storage
1. Medication state The clinical truth: what this patient is on, was on, and is planned to start. Patient level, continuously editable. The Medication record tab. Rows persist across visits and are never owned by an event. medications_cumulative_patient_state, cached one row per record event, built from medications_record_event + _entry
2. Supply responsibility Who we are relying on to get this drug to the patient from now on: Hospital, GP, Patient, or blank. An assignment we can make, not a supply we can observe. The Responsibility to supply column. It reads as a label, is changed in the Change dialog, and moves on its own when an order transfers responsibility. supply_responsibility on the entry. The dispense condition is not here: it belongs to the order
3. Generated artefact The prescription itself: a single dispensable order, signed by one prescriber, fixed at signature and released at issue (printed, or pharmacy has begun signing). The Prescriptions tab. An unsigned draft projects the record; signing snapshots it. Reprinting or reissuing never edits the record. medications_supply_artefact with lifecycle state, items_signed written at signature, and one prescriber signature. Items in medications_supply_artefact_item, carrying the dispense condition and location

Behaviours this prototype is demonstrating

BehaviourProblem it addressesTry it
Uniqueness by construction Duplicate medication lines from repeat events and same-day prescriptions Add a drug the patient is already on. The record refuses to create a second line and offers to edit the existing one.
Prescription decoupled from record Editing a prescription silently changing the clinical record, and vice versa Save an order as a draft, change a dose and watch the draft follow. Sign it, then change the dose again: the order keeps what was signed and reports the divergence.
Frozen at signature, released at issue Printed and dispensed prescriptions being edited after the fact, while a forgotten drug forces a duplicate Amend a draft freely. Once signed, the order stops following the record, and amending means voiding the signature and signing again, which keeps the same order number. After issue there is no edit and no re-sign, only cancel and reissue, which stay linked.
Divergence is flagged, not applied A pharmacist dispensing an order the prescriber has since changed their mind about, with no way to know Sign an order, then change that drug on the record. The order is flagged on its header, in its worklist line and in the change dialog. It does not block dispensing; it gives pharmacy a reason to query.
One prescriber per artefact Ambiguity over who is legally responsible for a prescription Switch role to Nurse. The Hospital prescription and FP10 buttons are gone; only Supply under PGD remains, and only for drugs her protocols cover.
Future-dated medications No way to record a planned start without it appearing as current The planned row shows as Planned and is excluded from the current list until its start date.
Duration, dates and taper on one dialog Today duration and taper are hidden until Prescribe is switched on, so "give this for two weeks" takes two steps and a reducing course takes three Open Change on any drug. Duration, the stop date and the reducing course are next to dose and frequency. Nothing is prescribed.
Changes commit with the event If the list is shared, a half-finished edit must not appear on anyone else's screen, and cancelling out must still mean undo Change a dose. The bar at the top counts unsaved changes and the row is marked. Save, and the marker becomes "changed at this visit". Discard, and the record reverts.
Ordering commits the record first Printing cannot be undone by cancelling a form, so an order must never be generated from changes that exist only in a browser Change a dose without saving, then press Hospital prescription. The record is committed as part of creating the order.
Supply responsibility moves at issue "Hospital to supply and GP to continue" stays on the row forever, when only the second half is still true Order acetazolamide on "Hospital to supply and GP to continue" and print it. The row stops saying the hospital supplies it and says the GP does.
PIN signs without a button An extra click on every prescription, and an interaction that does not match every other PIN field in OpenEyes Press Hospital prescription. The PIN field has focus; type any six digits and the order exists.
Eye relevance as a per-patient override [cod] misses systemic drugs that matter to the eye, and one drug-set default cannot be right for every patient Use "Eye relevant" on hydroxychloroquine under Other systemic. It moves group and is marked as an override of the drug-set default.
Dispense conditions are the existing lookups, on the order Supply is tied to the act of prescribing, and behaviour is inferred from condition names rather than declared Switch Institution to Gosport. FP10 disappears, because that mapping is not there. Responsibility to supply is unaffected, because that is about this patient rather than a local setting.
PGD supply is per user and per drug A PGD item lands on someone else's prescription under their signature (OE-18130) Switch role to Nurse. Add PGD set appears, and Supply under PGD is offered only on the drugs her protocols cover.
Quick add is preserved, but per drug A set add is a block of rows owned by one event, and can add a duplicate Add standard set, then Cataract post-op. The allergy and the duplicate are named before anything is added.
Planned stop or hold, with confirmation No way to record an instruction that has not happened yet, or whether it was followed Press Stop and choose "days before next appointment". The drug stays active until someone answers "did this happen?".
Reprint versus reissue Reprinting a copy that is already in circulation lets one order be dispensed twice Print an already-printed prescription. The advisory asks whether the earlier copy still exists rather than guessing.
Pharmacy worklist and query Outstanding work is inferred by counting signatures, and pharmacy can only decline to sign Switch role to Pharmacist. Sign the dispensing roles, or raise a query that blocks completion.
Order-level note No way to tell pharmacy why an order was reissued Reissue a prescription. The successor carries a note pre-filled from the cancellation reason.
Drug-level attribution and history Element-level audit only; cannot see who changed what Open History on any row to see the entry chain with actor and timestamp.
